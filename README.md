# CAD Laser-Cut Car Project  

This is a **Laser-cut Cardboard Car** that was designed to demonstrate 3D CAD modelling skills and creativity.  
Needed to be cut from one A3 sheet of cardboard and be assembled to be free standing. 
The project succeeded in these objectives.

### Tools Used  

- SolidWorks (CAD design)  
- Laser cutting (fabrication). CO2 laser. Kerf width of aproximatly 0.25mm for 1.3mm card.

### Modelling

The modelling was quite simple as it only included flat shape feature extruded to the thickness of the card itself.
Where the challenge lay was in the assembly and the accomodation of the kerf width tolerances considerations for the actual assembly.
This requrire a number of iterations and adjustments to get correct; Especially the angle of the bonnet and that scoop and the wheel seating height.
For clarity this is not a realistic design as the engine is located in the back while there is a scoop/intake thing through the bonnet.
But it looks awesome and I'm a mechatronics major; mechanical sins of infeasibility don't faze me.

### Laser Cutting

The Laser cutting involve taking every part of the assmebly and laying them flat on a plane to then generate the .DXF file.
This is linked to by the Lasercut file in the contents section.
This file is made to hold every part inside of the equivalent of an A3 section.
This is designed for a C02 laser with the aforemeationed kerf width.
One consideration when cutting: the axels part that slot together are on the thinner side and due to card inperfections spares of these should be printed.
This control can be done easily at the printer before printing.

### Physical Construction 

This does take a while to put together because of the number of parts.
Also depending on card imperfections it may be advantagous to have a pen to widen or push-together slots and fins respectively.
It does assemble together quite rigidly inspite of these considerations, and still holds up even a year later.
